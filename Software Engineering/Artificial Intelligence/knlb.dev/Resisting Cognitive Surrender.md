# [Resisting Cognitive Surrender](https://knlb.dev/essays/surrender)
* Author comments on how there is peer pressure / game theory in the work place around cognitive surrender - if any colleagues have seemingly surrendered then the author is only going to look worse spending too much time understanding what they're working on
* Author comments on how the volume of work _appears_ remarkably productive
* > Part of what makes me deeply uncomfortable is suffering from a form of Gell-Mann amnesia while working with agents. When I have expertise and the overall design direction I can generally simplify the output significantly, controlling for complexity. When I don’t they seem to be the smartest engineers in existence – far beyond the more squishy, organic variety – and all I can do is follow in awe. And then the agent gets stuck creating a directory and I have to reset all my assumptions.
* Author references a paper on automation that warns that it becomes even harder to debug agentic task failures because one never manages to get any practice with these tasks
  * Additionally, one doesn't get the chance to internalize what the system feels like under standard operating circumstances
* Author has a concept of a "vibe limit", which is the complexity of the change that the author can make with sufficient confidence
* Author identifies a warning sign of repeatedly tapping "yes, continue" as a sign that they either need to delegate the problem more completely to the AI agent _or_ that they've stopped thinking and need to check the agent's decision-making
* Author forces themselves to make all changes by hand
* Author asks agent to explain the changes being made to force comprehension
