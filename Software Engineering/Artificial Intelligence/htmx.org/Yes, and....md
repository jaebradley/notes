# [Yes, and...](https://htmx.org/essays/yes-and/)
* Author has a hard time understanding how problem-solving with computers and learning to control complexity when solving these problems is less valuable in the future
* Author thinks that the act of coding will lose relative value
* Author thinks while some business people look at AI and say "Great, we don't need programmers!", it is equally plausible that programmers look at business people and think "Great, we don't need business people"
* Author finds that most bad architects are those that were underperforming coders or didn't have much coding experience at all

## [HackerNews Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50003796)
* [One commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50019033) equates the artisanal craft of weaving beautiful Persian rugs with "hand-coded" software
  * > "Yes, there are still artisans making and selling beautiful rugs at premium prices. But most people now are content with resting their feet on a cheap Ikea thing that they can replace every few years, so that market has shrunk to almost nothing."
* [Another commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50016855) said

> "I am the engineer, I am the one that has the vision, and I am the one that needs to understand the thing down to the minute details.

> That said, most systems I still write are not so complex I need a very smart assistant, so I rarely use LLMs at all; it is still a valuable skill being able to research and think hard for yourself. An LLM cannot think out of the box (of its training dataset), and there lie the discoveries and paradigm shifts that move tech forward."
