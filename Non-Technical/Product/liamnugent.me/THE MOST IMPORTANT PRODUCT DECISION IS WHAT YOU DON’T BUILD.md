# [THE MOST IMPORTANT PRODUCT DECISION IS WHAT YOU DON’T BUILD](https://liamnugent.me/posts/what-you-dont-build/)
* Author uses example of a "document hub" product as an example of a lazy product idea that results in every stakeholder wanting to "add one more thing"
* Author argues that by the end, you've essentially rebuilt Google Drive, with tagging, archiving, priting, sharing, etc
* Illustrating the running costs, and not the build costs, is part of the "true" cost of a feature
* Resist the temptation of building a one-size-fits-all solution

## Nobody gets promoted for deleting things
* "Those who create and launch are the people who are rewarded and looked up to because we still have a culture that rewards the production of things over everything else"
  * Reviewing, maintaining, removing are all seen as lesser work
* Found that stripping roughly 80-90% of a site's content made companies sell more, cut support calls, help people find what they were looking for faster
* People systematically overlook subtractive change
* Additive ideas arrive quickly (cheaply)
* Subtractive ones cost real cognitive effort

## The elephant in the rollneck
* Steve Jobs' 1997 matrix was (portable & desktop) x (pro & consumer)
* Idea was to focus on these four products / personas and avoid misspending opportunity cost on other less-important products

## [HackerNews Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49746407)
* Ever-growing maintenance costs have the ability to annihilate team capacity
  * These growing maintenance costs can aggravate other scope creep issues
* [Commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49752245) mentions viewing product features into three categories: value-adding features, commodity/basic non-value adding features, and negative-value features (commodity features done poorly)
  * Example is sign up/in - one has to have it, and if done poorly, it removes value from the product
  * Commenter points out how many products try and innovate and can botch the experience creating onboarding friction
* [Commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49747864) mentioned how a feature request like "we want custom reporting" is usually because an application's built-in tooling for sorting/filtering/aggregating data isn't answering the users' questions
