# [Bug blindness](https://danluu.com/bug-blind/)
* Author's anecdotal evidence of using Blackboard (as a student) and not enjoying the experience, and then interacting with a Blackboard employee where the employee was oblivious to how their software was perceived
* Author observes that humans have a high ability to ignore negatives in things they're a fan of, including and especially, their own work
* Author has an anecdotal story about a defective mechanical mouse that he owned
  * Throughout the years, the author had gotten used to the mouse's erratic movements with "countervailing erratic movements"
  * The mouse was unusable by somebody that was used to normal mechanical mouse movement
  * Author uses this story as an example of their feeling that some (large) fraction of computer and software literacy is developing a (large) library of these habits / workarounds
* One way to overcome quality blindness is to have people dogfood their own software
  * Only works to the extent that people don't figure out habits that work around whatever issues the software has
* A large part of making an app easy for people to use is making these weird habits unnecessary
  * Reflexive reaction of most developers is "huh? It's easy to do X, just do {some complex sequence of things most basic users wouldn't think to do until they were a power user}" or "huh? didn't you see the instructions on page {big number} of the manual?"
* Author observes that teams with a high degree of quality blindness ship products that have reduced / no chance of success because of product quality issues
