# [On Being an Engineer in 2026](https://shahar.blog/2026/11/08/on-being-an-engineer-in-2026/)
* Author argues that (some) humans still have leverage over agents
* Author's argument, using proof by contradiction, goes something like
  * Assume humans have no leverage over agents
  * Agents are doing all the thinking and humans actually get in the way
  * If humans don't matter, then the difference between the 10x engineer's output and the 1x (or 0.5x) engineer's output should converge
  * Author argues that great engineers are still producing much better results than average engineers
    * Author actually argues that 10x engineers are becoming 100x engineers, while the average engineer is still average
  * Thus, there *must* be something that the human brings to the table
* Author tries to identify what leverage humans have over agents
  * Agents get lost when the problem isn't defined well enough, whereas (skilled) humans keep asking questions until they feel like they've solved the actual problem
  * Agents overcomplicate simple things or oversimplify complex ones
    * Skilled humans have intuition about which problems are hard and which ones are simple
    * Tacit knowledge - the idea that we can know more than we can tell or things we know but can't easily explain ("intuition")

## [HackerNews Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50426681)
* One [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50432187) mentions that over the past 10-15 years there's been a flood of engineers that entered the profession that are average
  * No interest in software development other than  "it's a cushy well-paid desk job with nice perks like WFH"
  * Now an agent can do the "just show up and implement a ticket" part of the job, so these average engineers are no longer necessary
  * So any engineer that isn't a 10x-100x engineer feels like an opportunity cost
  * Commenter finally says that all the 10x engineers they know have never been more excited about their jobs
* One [commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50431376) brought up "the sandwich model", which I hadn't heard before
  * The sandwich model: someone needs to say what to do, someone needs to do it, and someone needs to say if it's been done well enough
  * Even if AI can implement the "middle" part of the sandwich, commenter argues that humans are necessary for the first and last part of the "sandwich"
    * Replace humans at the start of the sandwich and the wrong thing may get worked on
    * Replace humans at the end of the sandwich and the output is slop
* [Commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50432339) mentions that customers are bad representatives for "what to do" pointing out that customers would have asked for a faster horse instead of a car
  * Customers also (rightly) hate being used for QA and quality issues need to be addressed before a customer sees it to form a durable impression of the product
  * Another similar example from [a CPA](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50435897) mentioned that if they did whatever the customer wanted, they'd be charged with tax evasion
