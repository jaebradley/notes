# [Why I Stopped Arguing With People](https://wangcong.org/2026-06-30-why-i-stopped-arguing-with-people.html)
* Correctness is not the same as goodness
* Being visibly correct means that someone may be visibly wrong
* When discussing pros and cons with smart, non-ego driven people, a disagreement is a joint search for a better answer - hopefully, both parties walk away sharper
* Discussing pros and cons with ego-driven people does not result in an answer, just a self being defended
* Most people feel first, then reason backwards to justify the feeling
* Most people don't learn from advice, they learn from consequences
* If you genuinely believe something others don't, that's a market opportunity
  * Instead of persuading a skeptic, ship the thing they think is wrong and let reality settle the disagreement
* Disruptive businesses exist because the founders believe something the rest of the world hasn't accepted yet
* You can only change yourself
  * Every hour spent trying to change someone who didn't ask is an hour "stolen" from the one person you can change (yourself)
  * Change yourself and the world will also change to match you
* Stop trying to win people over and start letting them be who they are
* You get better by continuously asking others for feedback - keep asking is the whole discipline
  * The ego that needs to win is the same ego that can't hear

## [From the HackerNews Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48746445)
* "Lincoln famously wrote and never mailed letters as a way to vent emotions."
* The key is understanding what the goal of any given conversation is - truth-seeking is just one possible goal amongst many
  * [Commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48751530) believes people care little about the goal of truth-seeking vs. other "goals" like creating shared meaning, understanding each other's values, building trust, etc.
* [There are 2 very different kinds of arguments. Arguments where you're trying to convince the other person. And arguments where you're trying to convince bystanders. These require completely different tactics.](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48748211)
  * If you're trying to convince the other person, be humble. Be gentle. Be subtle. Ask them questions. Let them think they came up with the idea entirely on their own. If any bystanders are watching this discussion, they are more likely to think that the other person is right, or that they are "winning". But this will give you the best possible chance of convincing the person you're talking to.
  * If you're trying to convince bystanders, project confidence. Present compelling evidence. Pick apart the other person's arguments and show why its flawed. Chances are, this will make the other person dig in even more strongly and resent you. But this will give you the best chance of convincing neutral bystanders.
* [The author’s point is that, even if you are correct 100% of the time, fighting every battle is toxic to yourself and everyone around you.](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48746681)
