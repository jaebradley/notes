# [Company Culture = Slack Emoji ÷ √Headcount](https://lia.mg/posts/measuring-company-culture-with-slack-emoji/)
* The culture "score" is the number of custom Slack emojis divided by the square root of the number of users
* The square root is to represent a non-linear relationship between organizations with orders of magnitude differences of users (for example, a company of 10k people would not be expected to have 1000x the number of custom emojis as a company of 10 people)
* **Score < 5** means that your workspace contains the default emoji set
  * Communication is conducted exclusively through the 👍 emoji
  * People are here for the salary
* **5 <= Score < 20** means a healthy, functional organization
* **20 <= Score < 50** means a thriving organization where `:this-is-fine:` has been used thousands of times in `#incidents`
* **Score >= 50** means a late-stage culture where every variant of `:thumbsup:` has subtext
  * Emojis begin to reference themselves (`:party-parrot-holding-a-party-parrot`)
* There is also a "culture leaders" score, which is the % of users who have uploaded at least one custom emoji
  * If less than 20% of users have uploaded at least one custom emoji, culture is being carried by a handful of people, and when they leave, they will take the culture with them

* [Commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50662397) mentions how one measure of culture could be the amount and variety of emoji reactions to messages
  * Commenter's anecdotal experience is that the best teams they've worked on have high response rates
  * In contrast, when there's a company-wide announcement and it only gets four generic emoji reactions, the cultural silence is deafening
